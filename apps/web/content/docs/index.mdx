Teitunnel puts anything running on your computer on the internet through
[Cloudflare Tunnel](/docs/concepts/cloudflare-tunnel), without opening a port on your router.
Share a local port at a temporary URL in one click, or publish it on your own domain with
DNS, logins and health checks handled for you. Every change to your Cloudflare account is
shown before it's made, and undone if a step fails.

## What you can do

- **Share** a local service at a `trycloudflare.com` address, or at a subdomain of your own
  domain for as long as you need it. See [Expose localhost](/docs/tutorials/expose-localhost).
- **Publish** routes on your domains: hostnames, paths, TCP, SSH and RDP, with DNS records
  created and cleaned up for you.
- **Protect** a route with a login ([Cloudflare Access](/docs/guides/require-login)) in one
  step.
- **Reach private networks** from devices running Cloudflare WARP.
- **Run several tunnels**, keep them running as a service, and **load balance** a route
  across machines.
- **Diagnose** problems with the [Doctor](/docs/reference/doctor), watch traffic and logs,
  and see what every change did in Activity.
- **Automate** with the [CLI](/docs/reference/cli), the [server API](/docs/reference/api),
  or export to `config.yml`, Docker Compose and Terraform.

## How it fits together

Teitunnel is a front end to Cloudflare's own pieces. It runs **cloudflared**, Cloudflare's
connector, which keeps an outbound connection to Cloudflare. It uses the **Cloudflare API**
with a token you create, to make tunnels, DNS records and logins. Nothing goes through a
Teispace server; there isn't one. If you ever stop using Teitunnel, your tunnels and records
stay in your Cloudflare account, and you can [export](/docs/guides/export) them to run with
plain `cloudflared`, Docker Compose or Terraform.
