---
title: Expose localhost to the internet
description: Share a website or API running on localhost at a public HTTPS URL with Cloudflare Tunnel, free and without port forwarding, from the app or the command line. An ngrok alternative on your own domain.
---

You have something running at `http://localhost:3000` and want someone else to open it: a
client reviewing a site, a teammate testing an API, your own phone. Cloudflare Tunnel does
this without touching your router. **cloudflared** on your computer makes an outbound
connection to Cloudflare, and Cloudflare sends visitors down it. Your IP address stays
private, and HTTPS comes with it.

There are two ways, depending on how long the link should last.

| | Quick Share | Route on your domain |
|---|---|---|
| Address | `random-words.trycloudflare.com` | `app.example.com` |
| Cloudflare account | not needed | free account + a domain |
| Same URL every time | no | yes |
| Logins, keep running as a service | no | yes |
| Best for | a quick look, a demo | anything longer, webhooks, sharing with a team |

## In the app: 30 seconds

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Start your app

    Make sure it answers at `http://localhost:3000` (or whichever port) on your machine.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Share it

    In Teitunnel choose **Quick Share**, pick your app from the list of running services (or
    type `3000`), and choose **Share**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Send the link

    Copy the `https://….trycloudflare.com` URL, or show the QR code to open it on a phone.
    **Stop Sharing** when you're done.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Screenshot name="quick-share" alt="A Quick Share of localhost with its public URL" />

For a stable address, connect Cloudflare and choose **Address ▸ On example.com** before
sharing; see [Quick Share on your own domain](/docs/getting-started/quick-share#on-your-own-domain).

## From the terminal

```sh
teitunnel-cli share 3000
```

The URL is printed first (with a QR code), and the share lasts until you press Ctrl-C.
Common variations:

```sh
teitunnel-cli share 8080 --for 1h                 # stop by itself after an hour
teitunnel-cli share 3000 --on demo.example.com    # your own domain, same URL every time
teitunnel-cli share https://localhost:8443        # an HTTPS dev server
```

## Make it permanent

When the link should outlive a demo, make it a **route**: a hostname on your domain that
stays until you remove it, can [require a login](/docs/guides/require-login) and can
[keep running](/docs/concepts/run-modes) when Teitunnel is closed. See
[put a service on your domain](/docs/getting-started/first-route).

## If it doesn't load

- **"Blocked request" or "Invalid Host header":** your dev server rejects unknown hostnames.
  See [dev servers](/docs/tutorials/dev-servers).
- **502 Bad Gateway:** Cloudflare reached your computer but nothing answered on that port.
  Check the port, and whether your app listens on `127.0.0.1` or `::1`; see
  [troubleshooting](/docs/reference/troubleshooting#502-bad-gateway).
- **It's slow:** Quick Tunnels are best-effort. A route on your own domain uses your
  account's tunnel and is faster and more reliable.

## Coming from ngrok?

The idea is the same: a local agent, a public URL, no open ports. The differences:

- It runs on **Cloudflare's network with your own Cloudflare account**: your domains, your
  DNS, Cloudflare's certificates, and logins through Cloudflare Access. There's no separate
  subscription for custom domains.
- Random URLs come from Cloudflare's free Quick Tunnels (`trycloudflare.com`) and need no
  sign-up at all.
- Teitunnel adds a desktop app, service discovery, a reviewed plan for every change to your
  account, and a Doctor that explains problems.
