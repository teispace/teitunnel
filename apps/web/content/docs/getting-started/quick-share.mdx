---
title: Share a local service
description: Get a free public HTTPS URL for anything running on your computer with Cloudflare Tunnel, in one click and without a Cloudflare account.
---

A **Quick Share** gives a service on your computer, such as a dev server on `localhost:3000`,
a public HTTPS address on `trycloudflare.com`. It needs no Cloudflare account, no port
forwarding and no configuration.

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Open Quick Share

    Choose **Quick Share** in the sidebar (⌘3 on macOS, Ctrl+3 on Windows and Linux), or
    **Share a Local Port…** from the menu bar or tray icon.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Pick what to share

    Teitunnel lists what's running on your machine: dev servers (Vite, Next.js, Astro, Django,
    Rails, Laravel and more) and Docker containers first. Pick one, or type a port or address
    such as `3000` or `http://localhost:8080`.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Choose when it stops, then Share

    **Keep running**, or stop by itself after 15 minutes, an hour or 8 hours. Choose
    **Share**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Screenshot name="quick-share" alt="Quick Share: a live share with its trycloudflare.com URL, request count and log" />

The URL appears as soon as Cloudflare assigns it, usually within a few seconds. Copy it, open
it, or **Show QR code** to open it on a phone. The card shows requests, errors and the
connector's log as they happen.

**Stop Sharing** ends it, as does quitting Teitunnel. Anyone with the link can reach the
service while it runs, so share only what you mean to.

## On your own domain

With a Cloudflare account connected (see [your first route](/docs/getting-started/first-route)),
the **Address** menu also offers your domains: choose **On example.com**, type a subdomain
such as `demo`, and **Share**. You get `https://demo.example.com` instead of a random
address, and it stays the same every time you share.

It's a real route on this machine's tunnel, made and removed through the same reviewed
plan as any route, but temporary: it goes away with its DNS record when you stop it, when its
time is up, or when you quit Teitunnel (and if Teitunnel crashes, it's removed the next time
it starts). It never takes over a hostname that already has a record Teitunnel didn't
create. It shows in **Routes** with a *Temporary* badge.

## From the terminal

```sh
teitunnel share 3000                          # a random trycloudflare.com URL
teitunnel share 3000 --for 30m                # stops by itself after 30 minutes
teitunnel share 3000 --on demo.example.com    # on your own domain
URL=$(teitunnel share 3000 --no-qr | head -1) # the URL alone, for scripts
```

It runs for as long as the command does; Ctrl-C stops it. `share` needs no account, only
cloudflared (the app's copy, or one on your system).

## Good to know

<Callout type="info">
Random `trycloudflare.com` addresses are Cloudflare's free **Quick Tunnels**. They're meant
for testing and demos: Cloudflare doesn't guarantee their uptime, the URL changes every time,
and Cloudflare limits them (for example, to a few hundred requests in flight at once, and
without server-sent events). For anything lasting, share on
[your own domain](/docs/getting-started/first-route).
</Callout>

- **Dev server says "host not allowed"?** Vite, Next.js, Django and Rails refuse requests for
  hostnames they don't know. See [share a dev server](/docs/tutorials/dev-servers).
- **Nothing loads?** Check the service answers at `http://localhost:<port>` on your own
  machine first; see [troubleshooting](/docs/reference/troubleshooting).
- **Webhooks:** a Quick Share on your own domain keeps the same URL between sessions, so you
  set it in Stripe or GitHub once. See [test webhooks](/docs/tutorials/webhooks).
