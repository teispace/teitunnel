A **route** sends a hostname on one of your Cloudflare domains, such as `app.example.com`, to
a service on your computer, such as `localhost:3000`. Unlike a Quick Share it has a stable
address, and it can [keep running](/docs/concepts/run-modes) after you quit the app.

## Before you start

- **A Cloudflare account.** The free plan is enough.
  [Sign up](https://dash.cloudflare.com/sign-up) if you don't have one.
- **A domain on Cloudflare.** In the Cloudflare dashboard choose **Add a domain**, then change
  the nameservers at your registrar to the two Cloudflare gives you. Routes on a domain start
  working once Cloudflare says it's **Active**, usually within an hour. Teitunnel shows domains
  still waiting under **Domains**.
- **Teitunnel** [installed](/docs/getting-started/install), and the service running on your
  machine: open `http://localhost:3000` in your browser to check.

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Connect your Cloudflare account

    Open **Settings ▸ Accounts ▸ Connect an Account** and choose **Open Cloudflare**. Your
    browser opens Cloudflare's *Create API token* page with everything Teitunnel needs already
    selected. Scroll down, choose **Continue to summary**, then **Create Token**, and copy the
    token.

    Paste it into Teitunnel under **Paste it here**. The token is checked with Cloudflare, then
    stored in your system's keychain (Keychain on macOS, Credential Manager on Windows, the
    Secret Service on Linux), and only ever sent to Cloudflare.

    <Screenshot name="accounts" alt="Settings, Accounts: a connected Cloudflare account" width={1240} height={1000} narrow caption="A connected account. Permissions shows what the token can do." />
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Start a new route

    Open **Routes** (⌘2 / Ctrl+2) and choose **New Route** (⌘N / Ctrl+N). Under **Service**, pick
    a detected dev server or container, or type a port (`3000`) or an address
    (`http://localhost:8080`, `https://localhost:8443`, `ssh://localhost:22`).

    <Screenshot name="new-route" alt="New Route: the service menu lists dev servers running on this machine" />
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Choose the hostname

    Type the subdomain (`app`) and pick the domain (`example.com`). Leave the subdomain empty
    to use the domain itself. Optionally, under **Advanced**, restrict the route to a path
    (such as `^/api/`), [require a login](/docs/guides/require-login), or change
    [origin settings](#origin-settings).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Review

    Choose **Review**. Teitunnel shows exactly what will change: the tunnel for this machine
    (created the first time), its routes, and the DNS record. If a record Teitunnel didn't
    create already uses the hostname, it says so and asks before replacing it.

    <Screenshot name="review" alt="Reviewing a new route: the tunnel, the route and the DNS record, with a warning about an existing record" />
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Apply

    Choose **Apply**. Each step is checked off as it runs; if one fails, the earlier ones are
    undone. Teitunnel then requests `https://app.example.com` through Cloudflare and tells you
    whether it works. The route shows as **Live** in Routes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What was created

In your Cloudflare account you'll now find:

- a tunnel named after this machine (**Zero Trust ▸ Networks ▸ Tunnels**), created with your
  first route and shared by every later one,
- a published application route on it: `app.example.com` → `http://localhost:3000`,
- a proxied `CNAME` record `app.example.com` → `<tunnel-id>.cfargotunnel.com` (**DNS ▸
  Records**), with the comment `teitunnel:route=…` so Teitunnel knows it made it.

See [routes and tunnels](/docs/concepts/routes-and-tunnels) for how these fit together.

## From the terminal

```sh
teitunnel route add app.example.com 3000
teitunnel routes
```

The CLI shows the same plan and asks before applying. See the [command line](/docs/reference/cli).

## Origin settings

**Advanced ▸ Origin settings** covers everything cloudflared can do when it talks to your
service. Leave a field empty for cloudflared's default.

- **HTTPS services:** accept a self-signed certificate (*No TLS verify*), the name on the
  certificate, a certificate authority file of your own, or HTTP/2.
- **Requests:** a different `Host` header (for dev servers that only answer to their own
  name, like some Vite and webpack setups; see [dev servers](/docs/tutorials/dev-servers)), no
  chunked encoding (some WSGI servers), or a SOCKS5 proxy for TCP routes.
- **Connection:** timeouts and idle connections, in seconds, and no IPv4/IPv6 fallback.

Settings made in the Cloudflare dashboard are kept when you edit a route here, and ones
Teitunnel doesn't show are never removed. Importing a `config.yml` carries its settings
over, including the ones at the top of the file that apply to every route.

## Keep it running

The route runs through this machine's connector while Teitunnel is open, including when its
window is closed and it's only in the menu bar or tray. To keep it running after you quit and
across restarts, turn on **Tunnels ▸ Keep running when Teitunnel quits**. See
[run modes](/docs/concepts/run-modes).
