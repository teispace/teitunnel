A **route** sends a hostname on one of your Cloudflare domains, such as `app.yourhost.com`,
to a service on your computer, such as `localhost:3000`. Unlike a
[Quick Share](/docs/getting-started/quick-share), its address never changes and it stays
until you remove it. It can [require a login](/docs/guides/require-login), and it can
[keep running](/docs/concepts/run-modes) after you quit the app.

**Use a route** for anything that should last: a self-hosted app, a staging site, a webhook
endpoint you set once, SSH to a home machine. For a link that should only live for a
session, a [share on your domain](/docs/getting-started/quick-share#on-your-own-domain) is
quicker and cleans up after itself. [Choose how to share](/docs/concepts/ways-to-share)
compares every option.

## Before you start

- **A Cloudflare account.** The free plan is enough, and Cloudflare Tunnel costs nothing on
  any plan. [Sign up](https://dash.cloudflare.com/sign-up) if you don't have one.
- **A domain on Cloudflare.** In the Cloudflare dashboard choose **Add a domain**, then change
  the nameservers at your registrar to the two Cloudflare gives you. Routes on a domain start
  working once Cloudflare says it's **Active**. Until then, Teitunnel lists the domain under
  **Domains** as *Waiting for nameservers*, with the nameservers to set.
- **Teitunnel** [installed](/docs/getting-started/install), and the service running on your
  machine: open `http://localhost:3000` in your browser to check.

## Add the route

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Connect your Cloudflare account

    Open **Settings ▸ Accounts** and choose **Connect an Account** (or **Use My Own Domain…**
    on the Overview). Then either:

    - **Sign in with Cloudflare**: your browser opens Cloudflare's consent page. Allow access,
      and every account the sign-in reaches is connected. Nothing to copy.
    - **Or connect with an API token**: **Open Cloudflare** opens the dashboard's
      *Create API token* page with Teitunnel's permissions selected. Choose
      **Continue to summary**, then **Create Token**, copy the token and paste it under
      **Paste it here**, then **Connect**.

    Either way the credential is checked with Cloudflare, stored in your system's keychain
    (Keychain on macOS, Credential Manager on Windows, the Secret Service on Linux), and only
    ever sent to Cloudflare. [Accounts and permissions](/docs/guides/accounts) explains the
    two ways and what each permission is for.

    <Screenshot name="accounts" alt="Settings, Accounts: a connected Cloudflare account" width={1240} height={1000} narrow caption="A connected account. Permissions shows what the credential can do." />
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Start a new route

    Open **Routes** (⌘2 / Ctrl+2) and choose **New Route** (⌘N / Ctrl+N). Under **Service**,
    pick a detected dev server or container, or type a port (`3000`) or an address
    (`http://localhost:8080`, `https://localhost:8443`, `ssh://localhost:22`).

    <Screenshot name="new-route" alt="New Route: the service menu lists dev servers running on this machine" />
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Choose the hostname

    Under **Public hostname**, type the subdomain (`app`) and pick the domain
    (`yourhost.com`). Leave the subdomain empty to use the domain itself. Teitunnel says
    whether the name is *Available* before you go on.

    Optionally, under **Advanced**: restrict the route to a **Path** (such as `^/api/`),
    **Require a login**, or change the [origin settings](#origin-settings). With more than one
    tunnel on this computer, **Tunnel** picks which one serves it.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Review

    Choose **Review**. Teitunnel shows exactly what will change in Cloudflare, and nothing
    changes until you apply: the tunnel for this machine (created with your first route), its
    routes, and the DNS record. If a record Teitunnel didn't create already uses the hostname,
    the plan says so, and you tick **Replace the existing records** or go back and pick
    another name.

    <Screenshot name="review" alt="Reviewing a new route: the tunnel, the route and the DNS record, with a warning about an existing record" />
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Add the route

    Choose **Add Route**. Each step is checked off as it runs; if one fails, the earlier ones
    are undone. Teitunnel then requests `https://app.yourhost.com` through Cloudflare and
    tells you whether *It works* (the connector can take a few seconds to connect the first
    time). The route shows as **Live** in Routes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## From the terminal

```sh
teitunnel setup                              # once: store an API token in the keychain
teitunnel route add app.yourhost.com 3000
```

The command prints the same plan and asks before applying:

```text
$ teitunnel route add app.yourhost.com 3000
 1. Create tunnel “laptop”
 2. Update tunnel “laptop” to serve 1 route
 3. Add DNS record app.yourhost.com → tunnel “laptop”
 4. Check https://app.yourhost.com works
Apply? [y/N] y
https://app.yourhost.com works.
```

`teitunnel routes` lists what's routed. `--allow ana@yourhost.com` adds a login, `--path`
limits the route to some paths, `-y` applies without asking, and `--replace` confirms
replacing a record Teitunnel didn't create. The command exits with status 1 if the check
fails. Every flag is in the [command reference](/docs/reference/cli#route-add).

`teitunnel route add` runs from the terminal, but the route itself is served by a connector:
the app's while it runs, or [`teitunnel up`, always-on or `teitunnel serve`](/docs/concepts/run-modes).

## What was created

In your Cloudflare account you'll now find:

- a tunnel named after this machine (**Zero Trust ▸ Networks ▸ Tunnels**), created with your
  first route and shared by every later one,
- a published application route on it: `app.yourhost.com` → `http://localhost:3000`,
- a proxied `CNAME` record `app.yourhost.com` → `<tunnel-id>.cfargotunnel.com` (**DNS ▸
  Records**), with the comment `teitunnel:route=…` so Teitunnel knows it made it.

The change is recorded, step by step, in **Activity**, and the confirmation that appears
right after applying has **Undo** to take it back. See
[routes and tunnels](/docs/concepts/routes-and-tunnels) for how these fit together, and
[DNS ownership](/docs/concepts/dns-ownership) for which records Teitunnel will change.

## What it needs

| | |
|---|---|
| Cloudflare plan | Free. Tunnels and their traffic cost nothing on any plan. |
| Permissions | Cloudflare Tunnel · Edit, DNS · Edit, Zone · Read and Account Settings · Read. A login adds Access: Apps and Policies · Edit. See [permissions](/docs/reference/permissions). |
| On this computer | The connector (the app, or `teitunnel up`) running while people use the route. |

## Origin settings

**Advanced ▸ Origin settings** covers everything cloudflared can do when it talks to your
service. Leave a field empty for cloudflared's default.

- **HTTPS services:** **Accept self-signed certificates**, a **Certificate name** when the
  certificate isn't for the hostname it's reached at (or **Use the visitor's hostname as the
  certificate name**), a **Certificate authority file** of your own, or
  **Connect with HTTP/2**.
- **Requests:** a different **Host header** (for dev servers that only answer to their own
  name, like some Vite and webpack setups; see [dev servers](/docs/tutorials/dev-servers)),
  **Don't use chunked encoding** (some WSGI servers need this), or a SOCKS5 **Proxy** for TCP
  routes.
- **Connection (seconds):** connect, TLS handshake and idle connection timeouts, TCP
  keepalive, idle connections kept, and **Don't fall back between IPv4 and IPv6**.

Settings made in the Cloudflare dashboard are kept when you edit a route here, and ones
Teitunnel doesn't show are never removed. Importing a `config.yml` carries its settings
over, including the ones at the top of the file that apply to every route. In the terminal,
each setting has a flag (`--no-tls-verify`, `--host-header`, `--connect-timeout`…).

## Keep it running

The route runs through this machine's connector while Teitunnel is open, including when its
window is closed and it's only in the menu bar or tray. Quit the app and the route stops
answering (visitors see Cloudflare's error 1033, or your [offline page](/docs/guides/offline-page)).

To keep it running after you quit and across restarts, turn on
**Tunnels ▸ Keep running when Teitunnel quits** (`teitunnel always-on` in the terminal). See
[run modes](/docs/concepts/run-modes).

## If it doesn't work

- **The check says 502, or "nothing is listening".** Cloudflare reached your computer but the
  service didn't answer. Check the port, and that the service is running.
- **Error 1033.** No connector is running for the tunnel: open Teitunnel, or turn on
  **Keep running when Teitunnel quits**.
- **The domain isn't in the list.** It isn't on the connected account, or the credential
  can't reach it. Check **Domains**, and the account's **Permissions** in
  **Settings ▸ Accounts**.
- **"This change replaces DNS records Teitunnel didn't create. Confirm it first."** The name
  is in use. Tick **Replace the existing records** if you mean to, or choose another name.
- **A dev server answers "Blocked request" or "Invalid Host header".** Set the Host header
  under **Origin settings**; see [dev servers](/docs/tutorials/dev-servers).

The [Doctor](/docs/reference/doctor) finds most of these by itself, and
[troubleshooting](/docs/reference/troubleshooting) covers Cloudflare's error pages one by one.
