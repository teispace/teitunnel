A login ([Require a login](/docs/guides/require-login)) decides **who** may open a
hostname. **Edge protection** decides what else happens to its traffic at Cloudflare,
before it reaches your computer:

- **Automated clients:** allow, **challenge** or **block** scripts, command-line tools and
  headless browsers. Verified bots such as search engines are let through.
- **AI crawlers:** block Cloudflare's verified AI crawlers.
- **Rate limit:** at most so many requests per period per visitor (IP address), then block
  or challenge them until the period ends.
- **Header rules:** set or remove request headers before they reach your service, and set,
  add or remove response headers before they reach visitors (for example
  `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` on a preview).

Cloudflare enforces these rules, so they keep working while this computer is off. Every
rule applies to **one hostname** only: Teitunnel never changes anything for a whole domain
(that's why it doesn't offer Bot Fight Mode or "Block AI bots", which are domain-wide
switches).

## Protect a hostname
Select a route in **Routes**, and in **Edge Protection** choose **Edit Protection…** (also
in the route's **Edit** sheet under **Advanced**, and the shield button on a share on your
domain). Pick what you want, **Review** the plan, and **Apply Rules**. The toast after
applying has **Undo**, which puts the previous settings back.

From the terminal:

```sh
teitunnel protect app.example.com                     # what it has now, and the quotas
teitunnel protect app.example.com --bots challenge --block-ai
teitunnel protect app.example.com --rate-limit 30/1m          # Pro plans and up
teitunnel protect app.example.com --set-response-header X-Robots-Tag:noindex
teitunnel protect app.example.com --remove-request-header Cookie
teitunnel protect app.example.com --off               # remove every rule Teitunnel added
```

Options you don't give keep their current value. `-y` applies without asking.

## Your plan's limits
Cloudflare counts rules per domain, and Teitunnel shows how many are used (yours and any
others) before and after each change:

| | Free | Pro | Business |
|---|---|---|---|
| Custom rules (bots, AI crawlers) | 5 | 20 | 100 |
| Rate limiting rules | 1 | 2 | 5 |
| Rate limit periods | 10 s | up to 1 min | up to 10 min |
| Transform Rules (headers, URL rewrites) | 10 | 25 | 50 |

Each protected hostname uses at most two custom rules and one Transform Rule per direction.

**Rate limits need Pro or higher.** On the Free plan the one rate limiting rule can't be
limited to a hostname (Cloudflare only lets it match paths there), so it would apply to
the whole domain; Teitunnel refuses instead of doing that. On Pro and higher, hostnames
with the **same limit share one rule**, so a second hostname doesn't use up the quota. If
the plan has no room for another limit, the plan tells you which hostnames use the
existing one and what it is: use the same limit, or remove it from them first.

## Rules Teitunnel didn't make
Teitunnel adds, changes and removes only its own rules, one at a time. They're marked
`teitunnel:` in their description, and it remembers their ids. Rules you or others made in
the dashboard are counted towards the quotas but never changed, moved or removed.

## Service tokens for machines
A login stops people who aren't allowed, and also CI jobs, scripts and other servers. A
**service token** lets a machine through with two headers:

```sh
curl https://api.example.com \
  -H "CF-Access-Client-Id: $CF_ACCESS_CLIENT_ID" \
  -H "CF-Access-Client-Secret: $CF_ACCESS_CLIENT_SECRET"
```

In the route's **Service Tokens**, choose **New Service Token…**, name it (for example
`CI`), review and **Create Token**. The client id is shown; the secret is **copied, never
displayed**, and only for the next 10 minutes: Cloudflare shows it once, and Teitunnel
doesn't keep it (it stores only the token's id and client id). Use **Copy Both Headers**
to paste them into your CI's secrets.

If the hostname has no login yet, it gets one that **only service tokens pass**: people
opening it in a browser are refused (the plan warns you). Tokens last a year. **Rotate**
gives a token a new secret (the old one stops working at once); **Revoke** takes it out of
the login and deletes it. Changing who may log in never removes the tokens.

```sh
teitunnel service-token create api.example.com --name CI   # prints both headers once
teitunnel service-token ls api.example.com
teitunnel service-token rotate api.example.com CI
teitunnel service-token revoke api.example.com CI
```

Service tokens are part of Cloudflare Zero Trust (free for up to 50 users; 50 tokens per
account).

## Permissions
Edge rules need **Zone WAF: Edit** and **Transform Rules: Edit**; service tokens need
**Access: Service Tokens: Edit**. The pre-filled token link asks for them; a token without
them gets the usual in-place fix that names what to add.

## AI agents
Agents use `get_protection`, `protect_hostname` (a plan, applied with `apply_plan` after
your approval), `service_token_list`, `service_token_create` and `service_token_revoke`
([AI agents](/docs/guides/ai-agents)). A token an agent creates is returned to it once,
after you approved it, and marked sensitive.
