title: Webhook inbox
description: "Keep webhooks (Stripe, GitHub, anything) while your computer is off, and deliver them in order when it's back, with a Worker and a D1 database on your own Cloudflare account."
---

Webhook senders give up after a few retries, so webhooks sent while your laptop sleeps are
often lost. A **webhook inbox** keeps them on your own Cloudflare account and delivers them
to your app, in order, once this computer is back.

## Turn it on
Open the route in **Routes**, and under **While This Computer Is Off**, choose **Add
Webhook Inbox…**. Give the path the webhooks come to (for example `/webhooks/`), how many
to keep and for how long, review the plan and apply it. The first inbox also creates a D1
database on your account (`teitunnel-data`).

From the terminal:

```sh
teitunnel inbox add app.example.com /webhooks/ --max 500 --days 7
teitunnel inbox items app.example.com /webhooks/
teitunnel inbox rm app.example.com /webhooks/
```

## What happens to a webhook
- **This computer is on and nothing is waiting:** the webhook goes straight to your app,
  as without the inbox.
- **This computer is off** (Cloudflare can't reach the tunnel, 530) **or the app doesn't
  answer** (502): the webhook is stored and the sender gets **202 Accepted**.
- **Earlier webhooks are still waiting:** new ones are stored behind them, so your app gets
  them in the order they were sent.

Only `POST`, `PUT`, `PATCH` and `DELETE` are kept; other requests (such as a sender's `GET`
verification) always go through. Bodies up to 512 KB are kept; larger ones pass through.

When this computer is back, Teitunnel delivers waiting webhooks every 30 seconds (or
**Deliver Now**), oldest first, with their original method, path, headers and body, plus
`X-Teitunnel-Inbox` (an id) and `X-Teitunnel-Received-At` (when it arrived at Cloudflare).
An answer below 500 counts as delivered; a 5xx or no answer stops the round and it's tried
again later. The route's section lists each webhook with when it arrived and when it was
delivered, and while the route is [inspected](/docs/guides/inspector), each delivery shows
there with its headers.

Signatures: the body and headers are delivered unchanged, so signatures still verify.
Senders that sign a timestamp (Stripe, Standard Webhooks) are usually checked with a
5-minute tolerance, so your app may need a longer tolerance for replayed deliveries.

## Only keep signed webhooks
An inbox can keep only webhooks with a valid signature (GitHub, Stripe or Standard
Webhooks), so nobody else can fill it. Save the signing secret for the hostname once
(the inspector uses the same one to check signatures):

```sh
teitunnel inbox secret app.example.com stripe   # reads it from standard input
teitunnel inbox add app.example.com /webhooks/stripe --verify stripe
```

The secret is kept in your system's keychain. Teitunnel sends it to Cloudflare only as a
secret of the inbox's Worker (never shown again, never in the plan or Activity).

## Limits and cost
An inbox holds up to 1,000 waiting webhooks (500 by default); when it's full, senders get
503 with `Retry-After`, so they retry later. Webhooks are deleted after 1 to 30 days (7 by
default), delivered or not. Every request to the inbox's path runs a Worker (100,000 free a
day for the account; the route fails open past that) and uses a few D1 rows (5 million
reads and 100,000 writes free a day).

## Permissions
**Account · Workers Scripts · Edit**, **Zone · Workers Routes · Edit** and
**Account · D1 · Edit**. If one is missing, Teitunnel shows how to add it to the same token.
