Teitunnel shows what this computer's connectors are doing (requests, failures, the round trip
to Cloudflare, their logs) and tells you when something goes wrong, even with the window
closed. For per-route numbers from Cloudflare's edge and uptime checks, see
[analytics, uptime and alerts](/docs/guides/analytics); for individual requests, the
[inspector](/docs/guides/inspector).

## Traffic

<Screenshot name="tunnels" alt="Tunnels: this computer's tunnel with its connector and traffic chart" />

Select a tunnel in **Tunnels**; its **Traffic** section charts this computer's connector:

- **Requests** and **Failed** requests per second;
- the **Round trip** to Cloudflare;
- over the last **Hour**, **Day** or **Week**.

Below the charts: **Now** (the current rate, such as *4.2 requests/s*), **Responses** by status
class (2xx, 3xx, 4xx, 5xx), **Since start** (*1,204 requests, 3 failed*), the number of
**Connections**, and the **Edge** locations in use (such as `AMS01`, `FRA08`).

**Overview** is the live dashboard: problems the Doctor found, then three tiles that open
[Analytics](/docs/guides/analytics): **Traffic** (requests per second and a sparkline of the
last hour), **Errors** (the share of the last 5 minutes' requests that got a 5xx or never reached
your service; orange for any, red over 5%) and **Uptime** (whether every checked route is up, or
which are down, and the lowest 24-hour uptime). Below your routes and Quick Shares,
**Recent Requests** lists the newest requests to inspected shares and routes as they arrive;
each opens in the [Inspector](/docs/guides/inspector).

While the chart is open it updates every second; otherwise the connector is sampled every 10
seconds. History is kept per minute for seven days, so the Day and Week views survive restarts.
The numbers come from the connector's own metrics on this computer: they count every request it
carried, and nothing is sent anywhere.

cloudflared reports traffic per tunnel, not per hostname, so these numbers cover all of the
tunnel's routes together. Per-route numbers come from Cloudflare's edge in
[Analytics](/docs/guides/analytics).

## Logs

- **Tunnels ▸ Logs** shows the connector's log: connections to Cloudflare opening and closing,
  configuration updates, and errors reaching your services.
- **Routes**, select a route, **Logs** shows only the lines about that route's requests.
  cloudflared logs failed requests; successful ones are logged only at debug level, so an empty
  log says *No failed requests. Successful ones aren't logged.*

The log viewer can **Show** **All**, **Warnings** or **Errors**, **Search** the log, **Pause**
and **Resume** it, **Copy** the visible lines, or **Save** them to a file. Secrets are removed
from saved logs.

The connector's log is kept in bounded files in Teitunnel's data folder, including for
[Always-on](/docs/concepts/run-modes#always-on) connectors, so it survives restarts without
growing forever. For a request-level view (headers, bodies, timing), use the
[inspector](/docs/guides/inspector).

## Machines running a tunnel

**Tunnels** lists every machine connected for a tunnel, with this computer marked
(**This Mac**): its public address, cloudflared version and the Cloudflare locations it connects
to. A route's **Machines** section shows the same for the tunnel that carries it.

Choose **Logs** next to another machine to follow its connector's log live (**Connector Logs**,
*Relayed live by Cloudflare while this is open*), with nothing to install there. It works for any
tunnel in the account, such as one running on a server, and shows warnings, errors and connection
changes. It needs the account's token to have the **Cloudflare Tunnel** permission (Edit).

If another machine also runs *this computer's* tunnel, the Doctor warns you: *Another machine
also runs this Mac's tunnel.* Cloudflare splits requests between the machines running a tunnel,
and each sends them to its own services, so some visitors reach the other machine instead of
this one. Find it with its logs and stop cloudflared there. When the other machine is supposed to
serve the same routes, that's [load balancing](/docs/guides/load-balancing) or
[adopting a tunnel](/docs/guides/several-tunnels).

## Notifications

Teitunnel notifies you when:

| When | Notification | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| This computer's connector loses its connection for more than 20 seconds, and when it's back | *Routes on this Mac are down* / *Routes are back* | **Routes down or back** |
| A connector keeps crashing | *The connector keeps stopping* | **Routes down or back** |
| The Doctor finds a new problem (it checks every few minutes, also with the window closed) | The problem's title, or *Teitunnel found 3 problems* | **Problems** |
| A Quick Share goes live or stops working | *Quick Share is live* / *Quick Share stopped working* | **Quick Shares** |
| A route goes down, errors or slows down | See [alerts](/docs/guides/analytics#alerts) | **Alerts** |
| A request hits a [watched path](/docs/guides/inspector#watched-paths-and-idle-stop) | *Request to /webhooks/stripe* | Per share or route |

Brief blips stay quiet, each problem is announced once, and nothing is shown while a Teitunnel
window is in front.

### Sleep, wake and network changes

Teitunnel notices when your computer wakes from sleep, comes back online or moves to another
network (another Wi-Fi, a VPN turned on or off). Connectors that were waiting to try again start
at once, and one still without a connection 15 seconds later is restarted. Quick Shares are left
to reconnect by themselves, so they keep their address. For 90 seconds after such a change,
nothing is reported as down: a connector that's still down after that is. A connector that kept
stopping while you were offline tries again when the network is back, and every 10 minutes
until then. Always-on connectors are restarted by your system's service manager. Turn each kind on or off in **Settings ▸ General ▸ Notifications**; alerts
also have **Quiet hours**. How notifications look is up to your system (on macOS,
**System Settings ▸ Notifications**).

## From a terminal

```sh
teitunnel status              # whether the app runs, and what it serves
teitunnel top                 # live dashboard: shares, routes, traffic, requests (q quits, ? for keys)
teitunnel routes              # each route and its status
teitunnel routes --check      # exit status 1 unless every route is live
teitunnel doctor              # the Doctor's checks; exit status 1 on an error
teitunnel traffic watch       # requests as they arrive, from the inspector
```

`teitunnel up` prints each tunnel's state as it changes (*laptop: connected*,
*laptop: connection lost, reconnecting*) and any [alerts](/docs/guides/analytics#alerts).
`teitunnel top` uses the running app when there is one; `--here` reads this computer's records
without it.

On a server, `teitunnel serve` shows the same in a web dashboard and its JSON API; see
[servers](/docs/guides/servers) and the [API](/docs/reference/api).

## Diagnostics

**Help ▸ Export Diagnostics…** saves a small `.tar.gz` with what's useful for a bug report: a
summary, the Doctor's results, your settings and the end of the newest logs, all with secrets
removed. The file list is shown before anything is written. Attach it to an issue with
**Help ▸ Report an Issue…**.

## Troubleshooting

- **The chart says *Traffic appears here a few seconds after the connector starts.*** The
  connector isn't running yet, or just started.
- **The Day or Week view is empty (*History appears here after the connector has run for a
  minute.*).** History is kept per minute; give it a minute.
- **A route's Logs are empty but visitors see errors.** The errors may come from Cloudflare
  before the request reaches the tunnel (a login, a firewall rule, a missing DNS record). Test
  the route, or look at the [Doctor](/docs/reference/doctor).
- **Another machine's Logs don't connect.** The token needs **Cloudflare Tunnel · Edit**; see
  [accounts](/docs/guides/accounts).
