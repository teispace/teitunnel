title: Inspector

The inspector sits between Cloudflare and your service on this computer and records each
request and its response while they stream through untouched:

```
visitor → Cloudflare → cloudflared → inspector (127.0.0.1, in Teitunnel) → your service
```

Nothing is sent anywhere: captures stay on this computer, and leave it only when you
export or copy them. Credentials (`Authorization`, cookies, API keys, webhook signatures
and token-like values) are **masked** everywhere unless you choose to reveal them.

## Quick Shares
Quick Shares go through the inspector **by default**. Turn it off for a share in the share
form (**Inspect requests**), or for every new share in **Settings ▸ Inspector**. Turning
it off (or on) for a running share restarts it, so it gets a new address.

Fixing a dev server that refuses the public address (**Send Host: localhost:5173**)
applies at once on an inspected share and keeps its address; a share without the
inspector restarts with a new one.

## Routes and shares on your domain
Routes aren't inspected until you ask: **Inspect this route** shows a plan that points the
route at the inspector on this computer (and remembers its own service), like any other
change. The route goes back to its own service:

- when you choose **Stop Inspecting** (also a plan);
- when Teitunnel quits (an [Always-on](/docs/concepts/run-modes) connector keeps serving,
  so the route is restored before the app exits);
- at the next launch, if Teitunnel didn't quit cleanly.

If a route is ever left pointing at an inspector that isn't running, the
[Doctor](/docs/reference/doctor) reports it (`inspect.orphan`) with **Restore the Route**.
Only routes this computer serves can be inspected; for other machines, use their
[live logs](/docs/guides/observability).

## What you see
- **Requests:** method, path, status, duration, size and time; filter by status class,
  method, path or host text and duration, and search text in headers and bodies (masked
  values can't be found by searching).
- **Details:** request and response headers and bodies (JSON, forms, text, images, hex),
  timings (connect, first byte, done), the visitor's address and country, and which
  answered: your service, a stub, a protection page or a simulated fault.
- **WebSockets and event streams:** messages in both directions with their size and a
  preview; event streams get a keep-alive comment after 25 s of silence so Cloudflare's
  100-second limit never cuts them.
- **Webhooks:** Stripe, GitHub, Slack, Shopify, Standard Webhooks (Svix, Clerk, Resend…),
  Twilio, Linear and Discord deliveries are recognised. Save the signing secret for the
  share or route and each delivery shows **valid**, **invalid** or **expired**. The secret
  is kept in your keychain, never in Teitunnel's database.

## Replay, edit, export
- **Replay** sends a captured request to your service again (up to 100 times), optionally
  edited: method, path, headers, body. With a saved webhook secret, **Re-sign** gives the
  replay a fresh, valid signature.
- **Export** as cURL, HTTPie, fetch, raw HTTP, HAR, JSON or Markdown (for an issue or an
  AI agent). Secrets are masked unless you untick **Redact**.

## Per share or route
- **Stubs:** answer a path with a saved response, always or only while your service is
  down (webhook senders stay happy while you restart).
- **Protection:** a password page, a secret link, basic auth, bearer tokens, IP allow and
  deny lists, and blocking AI crawlers or other user agents, enforced on this computer
  (works on Quick Shares too).
- **Header rules** and permissive **CORS** for development.
- **Network simulation:** 3G, 4G or satellite latency and bandwidth, and faults (a share
  of 500/502/503/504/429 answers, resets, slow first bytes) on matching paths.
- **Paused page:** keep the address, show a "back soon" page.
- **Watched paths:** a notification when a request hits, say, `/webhooks/*`.
- **Idle stop:** stop a share after some minutes without requests (off by default; set it
  in Settings or per share).

## History
The last 24 hours of captures are also kept on disk (at most 5,000 per share or route and
256 MB in all), so a restart keeps recent requests and the terminal can read them. Kept
captures have credentials masked when written, so they can't be revealed later and a
replay from history leaves masked headers out. Change how long history is kept, turn it
off, or clear it in **Settings ▸ Inspector**.

## From the terminal

```sh
teitunnel share 3000                   # inspected by default; a line per request
teitunnel share 3000 --no-inspect      # straight to the service
teitunnel share 3000 --idle 30m --watch '/webhooks/*'
teitunnel inspect app.example.com      # inspect a route until Ctrl-C (shows the plan first)
teitunnel inspect app.example.com --off  # restore a route left inspected
teitunnel traffic ls --status 5xx      # captured requests (every process's history)
teitunnel traffic get 7f3a9c1b --format curl
teitunnel traffic watch --path /webhooks
teitunnel traffic replay 7f3a9c1b --set-header 'X-Debug: 1' --times 3
teitunnel traffic export --har requests.har
teitunnel traffic clear
```

`teitunnel traffic` reads the history that the app and every `teitunnel` command with an
inspector write, so it shows their requests too. See the [CLI reference](/docs/reference/cli).

## AI agents
Agents read the same captures through Teitunnel's MCP server: `traffic_list`,
`traffic_get`, `traffic_replay`, `wait_for_request` (blocks until a matching request
arrives: webhook testing without polling), `traffic_stats` and `traffic_export`.
Credentials are masked unless you started the server with `--allow-secrets`. See
[AI agents](/docs/guides/ai-agents).

## Put an MCP server or a local AI server online
- `teitunnel share 8000 --mcp --on mcp.example.com` checks that port 8000 answers MCP,
  shares it on your domain (Quick Tunnels can't carry the event streams MCP uses), keeps
  streams alive, and requires a bearer token. It prints ready configurations for Claude
  Code (`claude mcp add --transport http …`), Cursor and VS Code. The token is kept in
  your keychain for that hostname, so clients keep working the next time you share it;
  `teitunnel token mcp.example.com` shows it and `--new` replaces it. Claude.ai and
  ChatGPT connectors can't send a fixed token (they use OAuth); OAuth in front of a local
  MCP server comes later.
- `teitunnel share 11434 --ai` (Ollama; also LM Studio on 1234 and vLLM on 8000) shares a
  local AI server behind a bearer token and prints the OpenAI-compatible base URL and key
  to use. Add `--on ai.example.com` for streaming answers.
