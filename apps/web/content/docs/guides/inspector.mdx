Everything stays on your computer."

The inspector sits between Cloudflare and your service, on this computer, and records each
request and its response while they stream through untouched:

```
visitor → Cloudflare → cloudflared → inspector (127.0.0.1, in Teitunnel) → your service
```

Use it to debug a webhook integration, see what a mobile app really sends, find the slow
request, reproduce a bug with the exact request that caused it, or test how a client copes with
errors. Captures stay on this computer and leave it only when you export or copy them.
Credentials (`Authorization`, cookies, API keys, webhook signatures and token-like values) are
**masked** everywhere unless you choose to show them.

## What gets inspected

Each inspected share, route or local domain is a **tap**: a small proxy in Teitunnel, on a
random local port, that your traffic passes through.

| | Inspected | How to turn it on or off |
|---|---|---|
| **Quick Shares** | By default | **Inspect requests** under **Advanced** when you share; the default for new shares is **Settings ▸ Inspector ▸ Inspect Quick Shares**. Changing it for a running share restarts it with a new address (the app asks first). |
| **Shares on your domain** | By default | The same switch, or `--no-inspect`. |
| **Routes** | When you ask | **Inspect this route** (see below), or `teitunnel inspect`. |
| **Local domains** | When you ask | **Record requests in the inspector**, or `--inspect`. |

### Inspecting a route

Routes aren't inspected until you ask, because it means pointing the route at this computer's
inspector in Cloudflare:

1. Select the route in **Routes** and, under **Requests**, choose **Inspect this route**.
2. The sheet *Inspect app.teispace.com* shows the plan: the route points at the inspector on
   this computer, and its own service is remembered. Choose **Start Inspecting**.
3. **Show Requests** opens its traffic in the Inspector. The route's section says *Its
   requests go through the inspector on this computer until you stop, or Teitunnel quits.*

The route goes back to its own service through another reviewed change:

- when you choose **Stop Inspecting**;
- when Teitunnel quits (an [Always-on](/docs/concepts/run-modes#always-on) connector keeps
  serving, so the route is restored before the app exits);
- at the next launch, if Teitunnel didn't quit cleanly.

If a route is ever left pointing at an inspector that isn't running, visitors get an error; the
route and the [Doctor](/docs/reference/doctor) say so (*app.teispace.com points at an inspector
that isn't running*) with **Restore the Route**. Only routes this computer serves can be
inspected; for other machines, use their [live logs](/docs/guides/observability).

## The Inspector view

Open **Inspector** in the sidebar. **Show requests from** picks one share, route or local
domain, or **All traffic**. New requests appear at the top as they arrive, with a count
(*248 requests*, *95% within 120 ms*).

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Find the request

    Each row shows the method, path, status, duration, size and time; replays, webhooks,
    WebSockets and event streams are marked. Narrow the list with:

    - **Status**: **All**, **Errors**, or a class (2xx, 3xx, 4xx, 5xx);
    - **Method**, and **Duration** (*Over …* a threshold);
    - **Path or host**: text in either;
    - **Search headers and bodies**: full-text search inside every captured request and
      response. Masked values can't be found by searching.

    **Pause** freezes the list while you read (*Paused · 12 new requests*); **Resume**
    catches up. **Load Earlier Requests** goes back further in the history.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Read it

    Select a request. The detail shows:

    - **Summary**: the URL, the share or route, **Answered by** (your service, the shared
      folder, a stub, the protection on this computer and why, the paused page or a simulated
      fault), the visitor's address and country, the protocol and Cloudflare's Ray ID;
    - **Request** and **Response**: **Headers** and **Body**. Bodies are decompressed and shown
      **Pretty** (JSON), as a **Form**, as multipart **Parts**, as a **Preview** (images), as
      **Text** or as **Hex**. Up to 1 MB of each body is captured (64 KB of it in the
      history kept on disk);
    - **Messages** for WebSockets and event streams: each message in either direction, with its
      size;
    - **Timing**: **Connect**, **Waiting** (to the first byte) and **Receive**.

    **Show Secrets** reveals the masked credentials of this one request until you hide them or
    select another. They're read on demand and never kept.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Act on it

    - **Replay** sends it to your service again, exactly as captured.
    - **Edit and Replay…** changes the **Method**, **Path**, **Headers** (one per line, as
      `Name: value`) and **Body** first, and can **Send** it up to 100 times. Headers you leave
      alone keep their captured values, masked ones included.
    - **Export…** copies or saves it (see [below](#export)).
    - Select two requests and choose **Compare Two Requests** to see what differs in their
      headers and bodies.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Clear Requests** forgets the captures of the selected tap (or all), including the history on
disk.

### Webhooks

Stripe, GitHub, Slack, Shopify, Standard Webhooks (Svix, Clerk, Resend…), Twilio, Linear and
Discord deliveries are recognised. Choose **Add Signing Secret…** on a delivery and paste the
secret from the provider's dashboard; from then on each delivery shows **Signature valid**,
**Signature invalid** or **Signature expired**, and how long before it arrived it was signed.
The secret is kept in your keychain for that share or route, never in Teitunnel's database.

With a saved secret, **Re-sign** in **Edit and Replay…** gives each replay a fresh, valid
signature, so you can replay a webhook after changing your handler without the provider.

### Export

**Export…** (one request) or **Export Requests…** (the selection) offers **cURL**, **HTTPie**,
**fetch**, **Raw HTTP**, **HAR**, **JSON** and **Markdown** (for an issue or an AI agent).
**Copy** or **Save to Downloads**. **Redact** is on: credentials are masked. Untick it only when
you need them, and the sheet warns: *This export includes credentials. Share it only where
they're safe.*

## Per share or route

Choose **Inspection Settings…** with a tap selected. These are done by the inspector on this
computer, so they apply only while Teitunnel (or the command serving it) runs.

| Tab | Settings |
|---|---|
| **Responses** | **Stubs**: answer a method and path with a saved status, content type and body, **Always** or **When the service is down** (webhook senders stay happy while you restart). **Paused page**: keep the address and show a "back soon" page with your heading and message. |
| **Breakpoints** | Hold matching requests (a method and path, such as `POST /webhooks/*`) before they go to your service, their answers before they go back, or both. See [breakpoints](#breakpoints). |
| **Headers** | Rules that **Set**, **Add** or **Remove** request and response headers; the **Host header** sent to your service; **Allow requests from any site (CORS)** for development. |
| **Network** | **Simulated network**: **3G** (300 ms ± 100 ms, about 200 KB/s down), **4G** (70 ms ± 20 ms, about 4 MB/s down), **Satellite** (600 ms ± 50 ms, about 2 MB/s down) or **Custom** latency and bandwidth. **Faults**: make a percentage of matching requests fail: **Answer with** a status code, **Drop connection**, **Delay**, or **Time out after**. |
| **More** | **Keep event streams alive** (a comment line after 25 seconds of silence by default, so Cloudflare's 100-second limit never cuts a long stream); **Record requests** (off passes requests through without keeping them); **Watched paths**; **Stop after no requests for**. |
| **Protection** | See below. |

### Breakpoints

A breakpoint holds a request so you can look at it and decide what happens. Add one in the
**Breakpoints** tab, or select a captured request and click **Hold Requests Like This**. When a
request is held, the list shows it as **Held**, a banner counts what's waiting, and the
Inspector opens it:

- **Continue** sends it on as it is. Change the method, path, headers or body first and the
  button becomes **Continue with Changes** (**⌘↩** works too);
- **Answer Myself…** (before the request reaches your service) sends your own status,
  headers and body, and your service never sees it;
- **Drop** closes the connection: the visitor gets no answer;
- **Continue All** in the banner lets everything waiting go on.

A held answer (the response side) can have its status, headers and body changed the same way.

A held request goes on by itself after a minute, so it's answered before Cloudflare stops
waiting after 100 seconds (a 524 error). It also goes on when the visitor leaves, when you
remove the breakpoints or stop recording, or when the share stops. At most 50 requests are held
at once; more go through. Bodies can be edited when they're text up to 1 MB whose size is
known; others (binary, compressed, larger, or still arriving) go on untouched. WebSocket
connections and event streams are never held. Breakpoints last until the share or route stops
being inspected; they're never saved, so a restart can't leave traffic stuck.

### Protection on this computer

The **Protection** tab puts a gate in front of the share or route, enforced by the inspector
(*It works on Quick Shares too, but only while Teitunnel runs*):

- **Password page**: visitors type a password once;
- **Secret link**: visitors need the link once, and a cookie lets them back in;
- **Basic authentication** with a user name and password;
- **Bearer token**: for API clients and AI tools that send `Authorization: Bearer`. The token
  is shown once;
- **Allow only these networks** and **Refuse these networks**: IP addresses or ranges;
- **Block user agents**: **AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot…)**, **Search engines**,
  **SEO crawlers**, or your own patterns;
- **Paths that skip protection**, such as `/webhooks/*`, so webhook senders still get through.

For a route, protection at Cloudflare's edge keeps working while this computer is off; see
[protection](/docs/guides/protection) and [require a login](/docs/guides/require-login).

### Watched paths and idle stop

- **Watched paths**: a notification when a request hits one of them (*Request to
  /webhooks/stripe*). Set them per tap, or for every share and route in **Settings ▸ Inspector ▸
  Watched paths**, one per line, with `*` as a wildcard.
- **Stop after no requests for**: a share stops by itself after this long without a request.
  The default for every share is **Settings ▸ Inspector ▸ Stop idle shares** (off by default).

## History

The last 24 hours of captures are also kept on disk, at most 5,000 per share or route and
256 MB in all, so a restart keeps recent requests and the terminal can read them. Kept captures
have credentials masked **before** they're written, so they can't be revealed later, and a
replay from history leaves masked headers out (*From history: masked headers are left out of the
replay.*).

In **Settings ▸ Inspector ▸ History**: turn **Keep recent requests** off, choose **Keep for**
(1 hour to 7 days), or **Clear…** everything.

## From the terminal

```sh
teitunnel share 3000                        # inspected by default; a line per request
teitunnel share 3000 --no-inspect           # straight to the service
teitunnel share 3000 --idle 30m --watch '/webhooks/*'
teitunnel inspect app.teispace.com          # inspect a route until Ctrl-C (shows the plan first)
teitunnel inspect app.teispace.com --off    # restore a route left inspected
teitunnel traffic ls --status 5xx           # captured requests, newest first
teitunnel traffic get 7f3a9c1b --format curl
teitunnel traffic watch --path /webhooks    # follow new requests until Ctrl-C
teitunnel traffic replay 7f3a9c1b --set-header 'X-Debug: 1' --times 3
teitunnel traffic export --har requests.har
teitunnel traffic clear
```

```text
$ teitunnel traffic ls --last 3
14:02:13  POST    500      41 ms    312 B  api.teispace.com/v1/orders  7f3a9c1b
14:02:11  GET     200      18 ms   2.1 KB  api.teispace.com/v1/orders?page=2  0c41d9e2
14:01:58  POST    200     204 ms     95 B  api.teispace.com/webhooks/stripe  a9e07b35
```

- **`traffic ls`** and **`traffic watch`** filter with `--host`, `--method`, `--status` (`404`
  or `5xx`), `--path`, `--kind` (`http`, `ws`, `sse`) and `--text` (anywhere in the request or
  response). `ls` shows `--last 20` by default; both take `--json`.
- **`traffic get <id>`** shows one request and its response, by its id or the end of it as `ls`
  shows it, as `--format markdown` (default), `curl`, `httpie`, `fetch`, `http`, `har` or
  `json`.
- **`traffic replay <id>`** sends it again, with `--method`, `--path`, `--body` (text, or
  `@file`), `--set-header 'Name: value'` (repeatable) and `--times` (1 to 100).
- **`traffic export`** writes `--har FILE`, or another `--format` to standard output, for the
  newest `--last 100` requests matching the filters.
- **`traffic clear`** forgets all captures, or one tap's with `--tap`.
- **`traffic openapi`** describes the API the captures show; see
  [OpenAPI from traffic](/docs/guides/openapi).

`teitunnel traffic` reads the history that the app and every `teitunnel` command with an
inspector write, so it shows their requests too. `teitunnel inspect` shows its plan and asks
first (`--yes` doesn't ask), prints requests while it runs, and points the route back when you
press Ctrl-C.

## AI agents

Agents read the same captures through Teitunnel's MCP server: `traffic_list`, `traffic_get`,
`traffic_replay`, `wait_for_request` (blocks until a matching request arrives: webhook testing
without polling), `traffic_stats`, `traffic_export` and `traffic_openapi`. Credentials are
masked unless you started the server with `--allow-secrets`. See
[AI agents](/docs/guides/ai-agents).

## Put an MCP server or a local AI server online

- `teitunnel share 8000 --mcp --on mcp.teispace.com` checks that port 8000 answers MCP, shares
  it on your domain (Quick Tunnels can't carry the event streams MCP uses), keeps streams alive,
  and requires a bearer token. It prints ready configurations for Claude Code, Cursor and VS
  Code. The token is kept in your keychain for that hostname, so clients keep working the next
  time you share it; `teitunnel token mcp.teispace.com` shows it and `--new` replaces it.
  `--mcp-path` sets the endpoint when it isn't `/mcp`, `/sse` or `/`.
- `teitunnel share 11434 --ai` (Ollama; also LM Studio on 1234 and vLLM on 8000) shares a local
  AI server behind a bearer token and prints the OpenAI-compatible base URL and key to use. Add
  `--on ai.teispace.com` for streaming answers.

## Troubleshooting

- **"That request isn't captured any more. Only the most recent requests are kept."** The
  capture was dropped from memory (1,000 per tap) or the history. Replay a newer one.
- **"The share or route that captured this request isn't inspected any more, so it can't be
  sent again from here."** Start inspecting it again, or copy it as cURL.
- **"Only web services (http:// or https://) can be inspected."** SSH, TCP and other routes pass
  through cloudflared directly.
- **"… isn't a route served by this computer."** Inspect it on the machine that serves it, or
  follow its [live logs](/docs/guides/observability).
- **"No captured requests: Teitunnel keeps them in the app's database, which doesn't exist on
  this machine yet."** Nothing has been inspected on this computer yet.
- **Requests appear without bodies.** **Record requests** may be off for that tap, or the
  body is larger than what's captured (*Captured the first 1 MB of 2.3 MB.*); requests
  from the history on disk keep 64 KB of each body.
