title: Analytics, uptime and alerts
description: "See each Cloudflare Tunnel route's traffic, errors and response times from Cloudflare's edge, check its uptime every minute, and get alerts when it goes down, errors or slows down."
---

Teitunnel shows how every route is doing in one place: how many requests it gets, which
answers it sends, how fast they are, where visitors come from, and whether it has been up.

## Where to find it
- **Analytics** in the sidebar lists every route of the account side by side: a sparkline
  of requests, the share of server errors (5xx), the 95th percentile response time and the
  uptime. Choose **Hour**, **Day**, **Week** or **Month**, and sort by any column.
- In **Routes**, the inspector's **Analytics** section shows one route in detail: uptime
  with a status strip, response times, incidents, a requests chart, responses by class,
  top paths, countries, browsers, verified bots and cache status.
- The traffic card on **Overview** opens Analytics.

## Traffic from Cloudflare's edge
Traffic numbers come from Cloudflare's GraphQL Analytics API, so they cover every request
to the hostname, whichever machine served it. They need one more token permission:
**Zone · Analytics · Read** (the token template also asks for **Account · Account
Analytics · Read**). If the token lacks it, Teitunnel shows how to add it to the existing
token, and checks again when you come back; nothing needs pasting.

What you see depends on the domain's plan. Cloudflare keeps a limited history per plan,
and response-time percentiles are available on Pro and above. When the plan doesn't offer
something, Teitunnel shows what it has and says what's missing. Answers are cached for a
minute (Hour) up to half an hour (Month), so the views stay well inside Cloudflare's limit
of 300 analytics queries per five minutes.

Precise counts from this machine's own connector are still in **Tunnels ▸ Traffic**.

## Uptime
While the app, `teitunnel up` or `teitunnel serve` runs, every route this machine serves
is requested through Cloudflare's edge once a minute, as a visitor would. Two failed checks
in a row open an incident; the next good one closes it. A route behind a login counts as
up when Cloudflare asks for the login.

- Checks are kept for two days and summed per hour for 30 days, so uptime shows for 24
  hours, 7 days and 30 days.
- Nothing is recorded while your computer is offline, and failures right after it wakes
  from sleep get another minute, so a closed laptop doesn't look like an outage.
- Only one process checks at a time: the app and `teitunnel up` on the same machine
  don't check twice.

## Alerts
Set them in **Settings ▸ General ▸ Alerts**:

| Alert | Default |
|---|---|
| A route is down | On, after 3 failed checks |
| A route is back up | On |
| Too many server errors | On, 5% of at least 20 requests over 10 minutes |
| Slow responses | Off, P95 above 3 s over 10 minutes |
| A connector loses Cloudflare | On |

Every alert is recorded in **Activity** (filter by **Alerts**). Notifications follow
**Settings ▸ Notifications ▸ Alerts**, and **Quiet hours** keep them silent at night while
still recording them. An alert fires once when a problem starts and once when it ends, and
the same route notifies at most once every 15 minutes.

## From the terminal
```sh
teitunnel analytics                      # every route of this machine, last 24 hours
teitunnel analytics app.example.com --range week
teitunnel analytics app.example.com --json
teitunnel uptime                         # 24 h, 7 d and 30 d uptime per route
```

`teitunnel up` and `teitunnel serve` run the uptime checks and print alerts. The server API
has `GET /api/analytics?accountId=…&range=day[&hostname=…]` and
`GET /api/uptime[?hostname=…&range=…]` (see [API](/docs/reference/api)).
