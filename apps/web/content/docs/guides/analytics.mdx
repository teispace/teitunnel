Teitunnel shows how every route is doing in one place: how many requests it gets, which answers
it sends, how fast they are, where visitors come from, and whether it has been up. Use it to
spot a route that started failing, to see whether a launch brought traffic, or to keep an eye on
a self-hosted service without setting up monitoring.

Three sources work together:

| | Where it comes from | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Traffic** | Cloudflare's GraphQL Analytics API | Every request to the hostname, whichever machine served it |
| **Uptime** | A check through Cloudflare's edge every minute, from this computer | Routes this computer serves |
| **Alerts** | Uptime checks, traffic and this computer's connectors | Routes this computer serves |

Precise per-tunnel counts from this computer's own connector are in
[Tunnels ▸ Traffic](/docs/guides/observability#traffic), and individual requests in the
[inspector](/docs/guides/inspector).

## In the app

- **Analytics** in the sidebar lists every route of the account side by side: a sparkline of
  **Requests**, the share of **Server errors** (5xx), the **P95** response time and the
  **Uptime**. Choose **Hour**, **Day**, **Week** or **Month**, and sort by any column.
  Routes served by other machines show *Not on this Mac* for uptime.
- In **Routes**, a selected route's **Analytics** section shows it in detail:
  - **Uptime** with a status strip (each slice up, down or *Not checked*), the uptime over the
    period, **Response time** through Cloudflare and **Incidents** (*Ongoing, since 14:05*, or
    when and for how long, with the cause, such as *Service unreachable*);
  - **Requests** over the period, **Sent** bytes and **Responses** by class;
  - **Origin time** (*P50 · P95 · P99*) and **Time to first byte**;
  - **Top paths**, **Countries**, **Browsers**, **Verified bots**, **People and other
    clients**, and **Cache** status.

  A label says where the numbers come from: *From Cloudflare's edge*, *From this Mac's
  connector*, or *From the local inspector* (when the route is inspected, the inspector
  answers first).
- The traffic card on **Overview** opens Analytics.

## Traffic from Cloudflare's edge

Traffic numbers come from Cloudflare's GraphQL Analytics API, so they cover every request to
the hostname, whichever machine served it, including requests Cloudflare answered itself (a
login page, a cached file, an error). They need one more token permission: **Zone · Analytics ·
Read** (the token template also asks for **Account · Account Analytics · Read**). If the token
lacks it, the section says *Traffic needs one more permission* and shows how to add it to the
existing token, then checks again when you come back; nothing needs pasting. Sign in with
Cloudflare asks for it when you connect.

What you see depends on the domain's plan. Cloudflare keeps a limited history per plan, and some
breakdowns, such as response-time percentiles, need a paid plan. When the plan doesn't offer
something, Teitunnel shows what it has and says what's missing: *Not on this domain's plan:
response times.* or *This domain's plan keeps traffic since …*.

Answers are cached for a minute (**Hour**), 5 minutes (**Day**), 15 minutes (**Week**) or half
an hour (**Month**), so the views stay well inside Cloudflare's limit of 300 analytics queries
per five minutes. If the limit is reached anyway, Teitunnel says *Cloudflare's analytics limit
is used up for a moment.* and tries again shortly.

## Uptime

While the app, `teitunnel up` or `teitunnel serve` runs, every route this computer serves is
requested through Cloudflare's edge once a minute, as a visitor would, and the answer is
recorded with its response time.

- **Two failed checks in a row open an incident**; the next good one closes it. Each incident
  keeps its cause: *No connector*, *Wrong tunnel*, *DNS record*, *Service unreachable*,
  *Service timed out*, *Server error*, *Certificate*, *Address refused*, *Limit reached*…
- **A route behind a login counts as up** when Cloudflare asks for the login.
- **Checks are kept for two days and summed per hour for 30 days**, so uptime shows for 24
  hours, 7 days and 30 days.
- **Nothing is recorded while your computer is offline**, and failures right after it wakes
  from sleep get another minute, so a closed laptop doesn't look like an outage.
- **Only one process checks at a time:** the app and `teitunnel up` on the same computer don't
  check twice.

Uptime measures the whole path (Cloudflare, the tunnel, the connector and your service) from
this computer's network. It's an honest picture of whether your route answered, not an
external monitor: when this computer is off, nothing is checked, and routes served by other
machines are checked there.

## Alerts

Set them in **Settings ▸ General ▸ Alerts**:

| Alert | Default |
|---|---|
| **A route is down** | On, after 3 failed checks in a row (*Checks before a route counts as down*) |
| **A route is back up** | On |
| **Too many server errors** | On, when 5% of at least 20 requests over 10 minutes are 5xx (*Error rate that alerts*) |
| **Slow responses** | Off; when enabled, P95 response time through Cloudflare above 3,000 ms over 10 minutes (*Response time that alerts*) |
| **A connector loses Cloudflare** | On |

An alert fires once when a problem starts (*app.teispace.com is down*, *app.teispace.com
answers with errors*, *app.teispace.com is slow*, *laptop lost its connection to Cloudflare*)
and once when it ends (*app.teispace.com is back up. It was down for 4 minutes.*). The same route
notifies at most once every 15 minutes, and several alerts at once are grouped (*3 alerts*).

- Every alert is recorded in **Activity**; filter by **Alerts**, or **Only Problems**.
- Notifications follow **Settings ▸ General ▸ Notifications ▸ Alerts**, and **Quiet hours**
  (**Quiet from**, **Quiet until**) keep them silent at night while still recording them.
- `teitunnel up` and `teitunnel serve` print alerts as they happen.

## From the terminal

```sh
teitunnel analytics                              # every route of this computer, last 24 hours
teitunnel analytics app.teispace.com --range week
teitunnel analytics api.teispace.com --path /v1 --range hour
teitunnel analytics app.teispace.com --json
teitunnel uptime                                 # 24 h, 7 d and 30 d uptime per route
teitunnel uptime --json
```

```text
$ teitunnel analytics
Teispace
  HOSTNAME                                   REQUESTS     5XX       P95   UPTIME
  app.teispace.com                              18204    0.2%     84 ms     100%
  api.teispace.com                               5530    1.4%    210 ms    99.3%

$ teitunnel analytics app.teispace.com --range week
app.teispace.com
  Requests      131840
  Sent          4.2 GB
  Responses     2xx 127311 · 3xx 3140 · 4xx 1206 · 5xx 183
  Origin time   p50 31 ms · p95 84 ms · p99 212 ms
  Top paths
       41022  /
       20310  /api/cart
  …

$ teitunnel uptime
ROUTE                                             24H       7D      30D       P95  STATUS
app.teispace.com                                 100%    99.9%    99.8%     92 ms  up
api.teispace.com                                99.3%    99.7%    99.7%    240 ms  down: The connector can't reach the local service.
```

`--range` takes `hour`, `day` (the default), `week` or `month`; `--account` (`-a`) picks the
account; `--path` narrows a route to requests under a path, such as `/api`. If the token can't read analytics,
the command says how to add **Zone ▸ Analytics ▸ Read** to it.

## Server API

`teitunnel serve` exposes the same data:

- `GET /api/analytics?accountId=…&range=day` for every route of this computer in the account,
  or with `&hostname=…` (and optionally `&path=…`) for one route in detail;
- `GET /api/uptime` for every route's uptime, or `?hostname=…&range=…` for one route with its
  status strip, response times and incidents.

See the [API reference](/docs/reference/api).

## Troubleshooting

- **"This account's token can't read analytics. Add the Analytics permission to see traffic."**
  Add **Zone · Analytics · Read** to the token; the app shows how, in place.
- **"This domain's Cloudflare plan doesn't include traffic analytics."** Uptime and this
  computer's connector traffic still work.
- **"Cloudflare's analytics limit is used up for a few minutes. Try again shortly."** Many
  views or scripts used the shared budget of 300 queries per five minutes.
- **Uptime says *Not checked yet*.** Routes are checked every minute while Teitunnel,
  `teitunnel up` or `teitunnel serve` runs; the first check comes shortly after it starts.
- **An incident while the route worked for you.** Look at its cause: *Service unreachable*
  means the connector couldn't reach your service at that moment, *No connector* that this
  computer's connector wasn't connected.
