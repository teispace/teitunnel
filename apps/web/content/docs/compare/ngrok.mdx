ngrok is a hosted tunnel service with its own network, a polished agent and many paid
features. Teitunnel is a free app for Cloudflare Tunnel: your shares and routes run on
Cloudflare, on your own account.

| | Teitunnel | ngrok Free |
|---|---|---|
| Price | free, open source | free; paid from $10/month |
| Traffic | no cap on tunnel traffic | 1 GB out and 20,000 HTTP requests a month |
| Endpoints online at once | no limit of Teitunnel's | 3 |
| Address | random `trycloudflare.com`, or any name on your domain | one assigned `*.ngrok-free.app` domain |
| Your own domain | free, once it's on Cloudflare | paid plans |
| Warning page for browsers | none on your domain | yes, until visitors click through |
| Inspect and replay | the [inspector](/docs/guides/inspector), in the app and `teitunnel traffic` | `localhost:4040`, with replay |
| Logins | [Cloudflare Access](/docs/guides/require-login), free up to 50 users | OAuth and OIDC |
| Desktop app | macOS, Windows, Linux | no |

## The warning page

On ngrok's free plan, a browser opening your address first sees ngrok's warning page and
has to click through; scripts can skip it with an `ngrok-skip-browser-warning` header.
A share on your own domain with Teitunnel shows your site straight away, which matters for
demos, previews sent to clients, and webhooks from services that load the page in a
browser.

## From ngrok commands

| ngrok | Teitunnel |
|---|---|
| `ngrok http 3000` | `teitunnel share 3000` |
| `ngrok http 3000 --url app.example.com` | `teitunnel share 3000 --on app.teispace.com` |
| `ngrok http 3000 --traffic-policy-file policy.yml` with an OAuth action | `teitunnel share 3000 --on app.teispace.com --allow you@teispace.com` |
| `localhost:4040`, **Replay** | **Inspector** in the app, or `teitunnel traffic ls` and `teitunnel traffic replay` |
| `ngrok.yml` endpoints | [`teitunnel.yml`](/docs/guides/project-file) in your repository, or [routes](/docs/getting-started/first-route) |
| an always-on agent service | **Keep running when Teitunnel quits** ([run modes](/docs/concepts/run-modes)) |

## When ngrok fits better

- Your domain can't move its nameservers to Cloudflare, and you need your own name.
- You use ngrok's other products, such as its API gateway and Traffic Policy features, and
  want them in one place.
- Your company already pays for ngrok and manages access there.
