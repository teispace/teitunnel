Teitunnel has several ways to make something on your computer reachable. They differ in how
long the address lasts, who can open it, whether your computer has to be on, and what they
need from Cloudflare. This page helps you pick one; each links to its full guide.

## The short answer

- **Someone should look at it now, and the link can die afterwards:**
  [Quick Share](/docs/getting-started/quick-share).
- **The same address every time, for a session** (webhooks, a demo you give often):
  a [share on your domain](/docs/getting-started/quick-share#on-your-own-domain).
- **It should stay up**, on this computer or a server: a
  [route](/docs/getting-started/first-route).
- **It's a set of files and must stay online while your computer is off:** a
  [Snapshot](/docs/guides/snapshots).
- **Only you, on this computer, but over real HTTPS:** a
  [local domain](/docs/guides/local-domains).
- **Only your team, and it isn't a website** (a NAS, a database, a whole home network):
  a [private network](/docs/guides/private-networks), or an [SSH or TCP route](/docs/tutorials/ssh).

## Side by side

| | Quick Share | Share on your domain | Route | Snapshot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Address | `quiet-river-lamp-orbit.trycloudflare.com`, new each time | `demo.yourhost.com`, yours | `app.yourhost.com`, yours | `preview.yourhost.com` or a `workers.dev` address |
| Lasts | Until you stop it, its time is up, or Teitunnel quits | The same | Until you remove it | Until you delete it, or its **Delete after** date |
| Your computer must be on | Yes | Yes | Yes, or run it on a [server](/docs/guides/servers) | No |
| What it serves | A live service or a folder | A live service or a folder | A live service: HTTP, SSH, RDP, SMB, TCP | Static files |
| Cloudflare account | Not needed | Needed, with a domain | Needed, with a domain | Needed |
| Login in front | Password page or secret link, on this computer | [Cloudflare Access](/docs/guides/require-login) | Cloudflare Access | Password or Cloudflare Access |
| Keeps running after you quit | No | No | With **Keep running when Teitunnel quits** | Always |
| Cost | Free | Free | Free | Free on the Workers free plan |
| In a terminal | `teitunnel share 3000` | `teitunnel share 3000 --on demo.yourhost.com` | `teitunnel route add app.yourhost.com 3000` | `teitunnel snapshot publish ./dist` |

Every one of them goes through a reviewed plan when it changes your Cloudflare account, and
none leaves a DNS record behind when it ends ([how changes work](/docs/concepts/changes)).

## Quick Share

A random `trycloudflare.com` address through one of Cloudflare's free **Quick Tunnels**.

- **Choose it when** you need a link in seconds, you have no Cloudflare account yet, or the
  address doesn't matter: showing a colleague a branch, opening your site on a phone, a
  one-off webhook test.
- **Not when** the address has to stay the same (you'd reconfigure every webhook), the service
  uses server-sent events (Quick Tunnels don't carry them), or more than a couple hundred
  requests arrive at once.

[Share a local service](/docs/getting-started/quick-share) · [limits](/docs/reference/limits#quick-shares)

## Share on your domain

A temporary route at a name you choose, removed with its DNS record when the share ends.
Names can come from your project and branch (`{branch}.dev.yourhost.com`).

- **Choose it when** you'd use a Quick Share, but the address should be the same next time:
  webhook endpoints you register once, a demo link you send before each meeting, a preview per
  branch. It can require a login and run on a [schedule](/docs/guides/sharing#on-a-schedule).
- **Not when** it should keep running without you: make it a route.

[Sharing](/docs/guides/sharing#shares-on-your-domain)

## Route

A hostname on your domain, permanently routed to a service on this computer's tunnel. It
stays until you remove it and can keep running as a background service after you quit the
app, or on a server with the `teitunnel` command.

- **Choose it when** people rely on it: a self-hosted app, Home Assistant, a staging site,
  SSH or Remote Desktop to a machine, an API a mobile app calls.
- **Add to it** a [login](/docs/guides/require-login), [edge protection](/docs/guides/protection)
  (bots, rate limits, headers, cache), an [offline page](/docs/guides/offline-page) for when
  the computer is off, a [webhook inbox](/docs/guides/webhook-inbox) that keeps webhooks until
  it's back, or [load balancing](/docs/guides/load-balancing) across machines.

[Put a service on your domain](/docs/getting-started/first-route) ·
[routes and tunnels](/docs/concepts/routes-and-tunnels) · [run modes](/docs/concepts/run-modes)

## Snapshot

A static copy of a folder, a build or a running site, hosted as a Worker on your Cloudflare
account. It keeps working with your computer off, and you can update it, roll back to an
earlier version, protect it and let reviewers [comment](/docs/guides/comments) on it.

- **Choose it when** what you show is files (a built site, a prototype, docs, a landing page)
  and it has to stay up without you: a client review over several days, a pull request
  preview.
- **Not when** people need the live app: its API, its database, logging in to it.

[Snapshots](/docs/guides/snapshots) · [previews from CI](/docs/guides/ci-previews)

## Local domain

An HTTPS address such as `https://shop.test` for a service on this computer, with a trusted
certificate. It never touches Cloudflare and isn't reachable from the internet; `.local`
names can be opened by phones on the same Wi-Fi.

- **Choose it when** you develop something that needs HTTPS or a proper hostname: secure
  cookies, OAuth redirects, service workers, several services on subdomains.
- **Not when** anyone outside your network should open it.

[Local domains](/docs/guides/local-domains)

## Private network or private hostname

People signed in to your Cloudflare Zero Trust organization with the **WARP** client reach
addresses on this computer's network (`192.168.1.0/24`) or a private name (`wiki.internal`),
as if they were there. Nothing gets a public address.

- **Choose it when** your team needs many things on a network, or things that aren't web
  pages, and you don't want any of them public: a NAS, printers, a database, internal tools.
- **Not when** the people who need it can't install WARP: use a route with a login instead.

[Private networks and SSH](/docs/guides/private-networks) · [SSH to a home machine](/docs/tutorials/ssh)

## Where it runs

Any of the live options (Quick Share, share on your domain, route) is served by a connector
on the machine where the service runs:

| Where | Use |
|---|---|
| The app on your computer | Everything, while the app is open (window closed is fine). |
| The app, with **Keep running when Teitunnel quits** | Routes that should outlive the app, and start at login. |
| `teitunnel up` or `teitunnel serve` on a server, in Docker or in CI | Routes that stay up when your laptop is closed. |

See [run modes](/docs/concepts/run-modes) and [servers and containers](/docs/guides/servers).
