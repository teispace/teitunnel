**Cloudflare Tunnel** connects a service on your computer or network to Cloudflare, so people
can reach it at a hostname on your domain without you opening any inbound port. It's free,
and it's what Teitunnel manages for you.

## How it works

```
Visitor ──HTTPS──▶ Cloudflare's edge ◀══ outbound connections ══ cloudflared ──▶ localhost:3000
                   (app.example.com)                             (your machine)
```

1. **cloudflared**, Cloudflare's open-source connector, runs on your machine and opens a few
   long-lived **outbound** connections to nearby Cloudflare data centers (on port 7844, over
   QUIC or HTTP/2).
2. A visitor opens `https://app.example.com`. DNS points the hostname at Cloudflare, which
   terminates HTTPS with its own certificate.
3. Cloudflare sends the request down one of cloudflared's connections, and cloudflared passes
   it to your service at `localhost:3000`.

Because the connection starts from your side, there's nothing to forward on your router, no
public IP needed (it works behind CGNAT), and your IP address is never published. Your
firewall can block all inbound traffic.

## The pieces, and their names

| Term | What it is | In Teitunnel |
|---|---|---|
| **Tunnel** | A named, persistent object in your Cloudflare account, with a UUID and a secret run token. | One per machine, made with your first route; more if you [ask](/docs/guides/several-tunnels). |
| **Connector** | A running cloudflared process for a tunnel. Several can run one tunnel. | This machine's connector; listed per tunnel in **Tunnels**. |
| **Route** (published application, ingress rule) | Which hostname (and path) goes to which local service. | A [route](/docs/concepts/routes-and-tunnels). |
| **DNS record** | A proxied `CNAME` from your hostname to `<tunnel-id>.cfargotunnel.com`. | Made and removed with the route. |
| **Quick Tunnel** | A throwaway tunnel with a random `trycloudflare.com` URL, no account needed. | [Quick Share](/docs/getting-started/quick-share). |
| **Private network** | An IP range reachable through the tunnel by devices running WARP. | [Private networks](/docs/guides/private-networks). |

## Remotely-managed and locally-managed tunnels

Cloudflare supports two ways to configure a tunnel:

- **Remotely-managed** (the current default): routes are stored in Cloudflare and changed
  through the dashboard or API; cloudflared only needs a token. Teitunnel creates these.
- **Locally-managed**: routes live in a `config.yml` next to cloudflared, which uses a
  credentials file. Teitunnel can [import](/docs/guides/import) their routes and
  [export](/docs/guides/export) its own back to `config.yml`.

## What it costs

Cloudflare Tunnel is included in Cloudflare's **free** plan, with no bandwidth charge for the
tunnel itself. You need a domain on Cloudflare for routes on your own hostnames (Quick
Tunnels need nothing). Some features around it are paid: Load Balancing is a paid add-on, and
Cloudflare Zero Trust (for logins) is free up to 50 users.

## Limits worth knowing

- **Request timeout:** Cloudflare waits up to 100 seconds for your service to answer (error
  524 after that) on most plans.
- **Upload size:** 100 MB per request on the Free and Pro plans (error 413).
- **Quick Tunnels** are for testing: no uptime guarantee, a limit on requests in flight, and
  no server-sent events.
- **Content:** Cloudflare's service terms apply. Serving mostly video or large files may
  need Cloudflare's media products; check the current terms for your plan.
- **WebSockets and gRPC** work through tunnels; for non-HTTP protocols (SSH, RDP, databases),
  visitors run cloudflared too. See [SSH and TCP](/docs/tutorials/ssh).

## Why use Teitunnel for it

Everything above can be done with the Cloudflare dashboard and the `cloudflared` command line.
Teitunnel does the same work in one place: it installs and verifies cloudflared, makes the
tunnel, routes and DNS records together, shows every change before it's made, undoes it if a
step fails, keeps connectors running, and explains problems with fixes. See
[how Teitunnel compares](/docs/concepts/compare).
