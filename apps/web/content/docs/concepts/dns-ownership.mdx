Your domain's DNS is shared with everything else you run: your website, email, verification
records, other people's tunnels. A tool that creates records also needs to know which ones
it may remove later, and a mistake there takes down something unrelated. So Teitunnel keeps
track of exactly which records it created, and treats every other record as yours.

## The rule

- **Records Teitunnel created** are changed and deleted as part of normal changes: removing a
  route removes its record, deleting a tunnel removes the records it made for it.
- **Every other record** is never changed or deleted without your explicit confirmation, and
  never deleted automatically. When a change would replace or delete one, the plan shows the
  record as it is (type and content) and waits for you to confirm.
- **Removing a route whose record Teitunnel didn't create** leaves the record in place, and
  the plan says so: *The DNS record for app.teispace.com wasn't created by Teitunnel, so it's
  left in place.*

The same rule covers the other things Teitunnel creates in your account: logins (Access
applications named *Teitunnel · app.teispace.com*), edge rules, service tokens and Workers.
A login someone else set up on the same hostname is never changed; the plan stops and says
to change it in the Cloudflare dashboard.

## How Teitunnel knows a record is its own

Two ways, so ownership survives a lost database or a new computer:

1. **A comment on the record.** Every record Teitunnel writes carries a DNS comment that
   starts with `teitunnel:`, for example:

   ```text
   teitunnel:route=3f9a1c07e2b4;by=<user>@<computer>
   ```

   `route=` is the route's id (derived from its hostname and path), and `by=` names who made
   it: the user and computer, or the value of `TEITUNNEL_OWNER` when it's set (CI jobs use
   a stable name). You can see these comments in the Cloudflare dashboard's DNS list.
2. **A local index.** Teitunnel also remembers the id of each record it created in its
   database on this computer, in case the comment is edited away.

A record counts as Teitunnel's if either says so. The index moves with an
[encrypted backup](/docs/guides/move-computer), and the comments mean a fresh install
recognises its records even without one.

<Callout type="warn">
Don't add a `teitunnel:` comment to your own records by hand: Teitunnel would treat them as
its own, and could replace or delete them without asking.
</Callout>

## Confirming a change to someone else's record

When you add a route on a hostname that already has a record Teitunnel didn't create (an old
`A` record for a server, a `CNAME` to another service), the plan shows it:

> app.teispace.com already has a DNS record (A 203.0.113.4) that Teitunnel didn't create. It
> will be replaced.

In the app, tick **Replace the existing records** in the plan before applying. Nothing
changes until you do; if you'd rather keep the record, pick another hostname.

From a terminal, the command stops before applying:

```text
$ teitunnel route add app.teispace.com 3000
! app.teispace.com already has an A record (203.0.113.4) that Teitunnel didn't create. It will be replaced.
 1. Update tunnel “laptop” to serve 2 routes
 2. Point app.teispace.com at tunnel “laptop” (was A 203.0.113.4)
 3. Check https://app.teispace.com works
teitunnel: This needs a confirmation (see above). Pass --replace to allow it.

$ teitunnel route add app.teispace.com 3000 --replace
```

`--replace` works the same on every command that changes Cloudflare (`route add`,
`route remove`, `project apply`, `up`, `tunnel delete`…). Even with it, the replaced record
is recorded in [Activity](/docs/concepts/changes#activity) with its old value, and if the
change fails, the old record is put back.

Some flows never replace a record at all: a
[share on your domain](/docs/guides/sharing) refuses a hostname with a record Teitunnel
didn't create, and so does the **Doctor**'s **Fix Safe Issues**.

## Other machines and teammates

Several people and machines often share one Cloudflare account. Since each record's comment
names who made it, Teitunnel knows when a hostname is **held** by someone else:

- **Another machine's route:** the hostname points at another tunnel, through a record
  another Teitunnel made. Adding the same hostname here would send its visitors to you. The
  plan says *app.teispace.com is routed by … on another machine. Going ahead takes it over,
  and their route stops working.*
- **A reservation** (below) that hasn't ended.

Taking a held hostname needs its own confirmation: **Take this hostname from whoever holds
it** in the app, `--take-over` in the terminal (without it, the command exits with status
3). Your own routes from earlier runs (the same owner through another tunnel, such as a
previous CI job) count as yours.

## Reservations

A **reservation** claims a hostname for you without routing it yet, so teammates see it's
taken: a feature branch's preview name, a demo you'll run on Friday.

- **App:** **Domains**, select the domain, **Reservations ▸ Reserve**. Enter the hostname
  and, optionally, **Until** a date. The list shows every reservation on the domain with
  its owner, end date, and whether it's **Also routed**; **Release** frees one of yours.
- **Terminal:**

  ```sh
  teitunnel reserve preview.teispace.com --until 2026-12-31   # end of that day, UTC
  teitunnel reservations                                      # who holds what
  teitunnel release preview.teispace.com
  ```

  ```text
  $ teitunnel reservations
  preview.teispace.com	you	until 2027-01-01T00:00Z
  demo.teispace.com	<user>@<computer>	no end date	routes it
  ```

A reservation is a real DNS record, so everyone on the account sees it, in Teitunnel and in
the Cloudflare dashboard: a proxied `AAAA` record to `100::` (an address reserved for
discarding traffic) with a comment like `teitunnel:lease;by=…;until=2027-01-01T00:00Z`. It
serves nothing, costs one record, and Cloudflare refuses a conflicting record on the name.

- Reserving it again changes the end date.
- A route you add on your own reserved name keeps the reservation; removing the route puts the
  placeholder back.
- An ended reservation frees the name; the placeholder is cleaned up on the next change.
- A hostname with a record Teitunnel didn't create can't be reserved: *… has a DNS record
  Teitunnel didn't create, so it can't be reserved. Choose another name.*

## Dangling records and the Doctor

The [Doctor](/docs/reference/doctor) looks for records that point at a tunnel but lead
nowhere:

- *app.teispace.com points at a tunnel that no longer exists.* Visitors get a Cloudflare error.
- *app.teispace.com points at this Mac's tunnel but has no route.* Visitors get a 404 from the
  tunnel.

A record left pointing at a deleted tunnel is also a risk, so both come with **Delete the
Record**. **Fix Safe Issues** (and `teitunnel doctor --fix`) only applies fixes whose plan
needs no confirmation, which means it only ever deletes records Teitunnel created. For
anything else it offers the fix and shows the plan, with the record, for you to confirm.
